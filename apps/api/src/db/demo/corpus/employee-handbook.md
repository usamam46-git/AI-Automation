# Employee Handbook

**Northwind Operations Ltd — People Team**
Document reference: HR-HB-001
Version 9.0 · Effective 1 April 2026 · Owner: Head of People

---

## 1. About this handbook

This handbook describes how we work together: the terms that apply to everyone,
the things you are entitled to, and the things we expect. It sits alongside your
individual contract of employment. Where the two differ, your contract governs
the specifics of your role, pay and notice, and this handbook governs everything
else.

The handbook is reviewed annually. Material changes are announced at least 30
days before they take effect.

## 2. Working hours and patterns

Standard full-time hours are **37.5 hours per week**, normally worked Monday to
Friday. There is no fixed start time; the expectation is that you are reachable
during the core hours of **10:00 to 16:00** in your own time zone, and that you
agree a consistent pattern with your team.

Part-time and compressed patterns are available on request and are agreed with
your line manager and the People Team. A change to your working pattern is
recorded as a contract variation.

Overtime is not paid for salaried roles. Where sustained additional hours are
required to meet a deadline, time off in lieu is agreed in advance with your line
manager and taken within three months.

## 3. Remote and hybrid working

We are hybrid by default. Most roles are expected in the office **two days per
week**, with the specific days agreed within the team rather than mandated
centrally. Some roles — facilities, reception, and lab-based work — are
site-based and are described as such in the job description.

Fully remote arrangements are approved case by case by the Head of People, and
are reviewed every twelve months.

Working from another country for more than 20 working days in a rolling year
needs prior approval, because it creates tax and social security obligations for
both you and the company. Ask before you book, not afterwards.

## 4. Annual leave

The annual leave entitlement is **25 working days per calendar year** in addition
to public holidays in your location. Entitlement accrues monthly and is pro-rated
for part-time employees and for anyone joining or leaving part-way through the
year.

### 4.1 Booking leave

Leave is requested through the HR system and requires line manager approval.
Requests of five working days or more should be made at least four weeks in
advance. Shorter requests are normally approved within two working days.

A request is assessed on team coverage, not on a first-come basis alone. Where
two requests conflict, the earlier request is usually approved, but a manager may
ask one of you to move for a business reason and should explain what it is.

### 4.2 Carryover

Up to **5 working days** may be carried into the following calendar year, and
must be taken by **31 March**. Days not taken by that date are lost. Carryover
above 5 days is approved only where leave was refused for a business reason, and
requires Head of People sign-off.

### 4.3 Leave on exit

On leaving, accrued and untaken leave is paid in the final salary payment. Leave
taken in excess of accrual is deducted from the final payment.

### 4.4 Worked example

An employee with the full 25-day entitlement who has taken 7.5 days by the end of
August, and who then books five working days from 7 to 11 September, will have a
remaining balance of 12.5 days for the rest of the year.

## 5. Sick leave

Tell your line manager as early as you can on the first day of absence, and keep
them updated. You do not need to explain the medical detail.

Company sick pay is **full pay for up to 20 working days** in a rolling
twelve-month period, then statutory sick pay in your jurisdiction. A self
certificate covers absences of up to seven calendar days; beyond that a medical
certificate is required.

Sickness during booked annual leave may be converted back to sick leave with a
medical certificate, and the annual leave days are returned to your balance.

## 6. Other leave

- **Parental leave** — statutory entitlement in your jurisdiction, enhanced to 26
  weeks at full pay for the primary caregiver and 8 weeks at full pay for the
  secondary caregiver, after 12 months' service.
- **Bereavement leave** — up to 5 days' paid leave on the death of an immediate
  family member, and up to 2 days otherwise. This is a floor, not a ceiling;
  talk to your manager.
- **Compassionate and emergency dependant leave** — up to 3 paid days per year
  for an unforeseen emergency involving a dependant.
- **Study leave** — up to 5 days per year for an approved professional
  qualification, plus the examination days themselves.
- **Jury service** — paid leave for the duration, with any court allowance offset
  against pay.

## 7. Probation and notice

New employees serve a **probationary period of three months**, extendable once by
a further three months where more time is genuinely needed to assess the role.
During probation the notice period is **one week** on either side.

After probation, notice periods are as stated in your contract. Unless your
contract says otherwise:

| Role level | Notice from employee | Notice from company |
|---|---|---|
| Individual contributor | 1 month | 1 month |
| Manager | 2 months | 2 months |
| Director and above | 3 months | 3 months |

The company may pay in lieu of notice. Garden leave may be used where you are
moving to a competitor.

## 8. Pay and review

Salaries are paid monthly, on the **last working day of the month**, in arrears.
Payslips are available in the HR system from two working days before payday.

Pay is reviewed annually, effective 1 September, against role, performance and
market benchmark. A review is not a guarantee of an increase. Off-cycle changes
are made where someone's responsibilities change materially, and require Head of
People approval.

## 9. Performance and development

Every employee has a documented objective set, reviewed at least twice a year in
a conversation that is recorded in the HR system. Feedback is expected to be
continuous rather than saved for those two conversations.

Each employee has a learning budget of **USD 1,500 per calendar year** for
courses, conferences and books relevant to their role. It does not carry over.
Expenses against it follow the Employee Expense Reimbursement Policy
(FIN-EXP-002).

## 10. Conduct

We expect honesty, respect, and care with the company's and our customers' data.
Specifically:

- Do not share credentials, and use the company password manager.
- Do not move customer data outside approved systems.
- Declare any outside interest that could conflict with your work, including paid
  advisory roles and directorships.
- Gifts and hospitality from suppliers above USD 100.00 in value must be declared
  to the People Team and may need to be declined.

Bullying, harassment and discrimination are treated as gross misconduct.

## 11. Raising a concern

Speak to your line manager first where you can. If that is not appropriate, go to
any member of the People Team, or to the confidential reporting line described on
the intranet.

Concerns raised in good faith are protected. Retaliation against someone who has
raised a concern is itself gross misconduct, regardless of whether the original
concern was upheld.

## 12. Leaving

Resignations are given in writing to your line manager. We will always offer an
exit conversation with the People Team; it is optional and what you say in it is
not shared with your manager in attributable form.

Return company equipment on or before your last working day. Access to systems is
withdrawn at the end of your last working day, so download anything personal
before then.
